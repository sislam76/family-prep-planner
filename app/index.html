How prepared, and where it'll live

Minimum covers the basics only. Comfortable adds extras your space allows (e.g. UHT milk, a solar panel, snacks). Thorough includes everything regardless of space. This is the biggest lever on total cost — start at Minimum if budget is tight, you can always add more later.

"Inside the house" means a cupboard, wardrobe or under the stairs. Lofts and garages/sheds swing hot and cold and can get damp — that damages medication, batteries, formula and some food. We'll flag items that shouldn't go out there.

UK guidance targets about 72 hours. For comparison, Sweden's MSB and Germany's BBK advise 1-2 weeks of self-sufficiency, and Nordic civil defence guidance (Norway, Finland) targets 3 days minimum, with some now recommending up to a week. Pick what feels realistic for you — longer just means more stock.

Food

What food would your household actually want to eat during a disruption? (select all that apply)

Military-style ration packs and plain tinned food cover calories, but in a multi-day disruption, familiar food matters for comfort and for kids actually eating — we'll suggest shelf-stable staples to match. Pick more than one if your household eats a mix (e.g. Western + South Asian). South Asian: rice, lentils/daal, ghee. Middle Eastern: rice, dates, chickpeas. East/Southeast Asian: rice, noodles, soy sauce. Caribbean/African: rice, beans, plantain.

Guidance adapted from UK Government household emergency preparedness advice (gov.uk "Prepare" campaign), cross-checked against official guidance from Sweden (MSB), Germany (BBK), Norway (DSB) and Finland (72tuntia.fi). This planner is for general household resilience against short supply-chain disruptions (power, water, gas, fuel, food) — not for large-scale or long-term catastrophic scenarios. We deliberately leave out items like iodine tablets that other countries list only for nuclear incidents, which is outside this planner's scope.
